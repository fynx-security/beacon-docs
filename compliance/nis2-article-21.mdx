The **NIS2 Directive** (EU 2022/2555) requires essential and important entities to implement risk management measures — **Article 21** lists ten thematic areas of cybersecurity.

Beacon maps automated evidence to a **simplified subset** of five controls suitable for continuous posture monitoring. This is a **product mapping**, not legal advice.

## Article 21 themes (summary)

| # | Theme |
|---|-------|
| 1 | Policies on risk analysis and information system security |
| 2 | Incident handling |
| 3 | Business continuity and crisis management |
| 4 | Supply chain security |
| 5 | Security in acquisition, development, maintenance |
| 6 | Policies to assess effectiveness of measures |
| 7 | Basic cyber hygiene and training |
| 8 | Cryptography and encryption |
| 9 | Human resources security, access control, asset management |
| 10 | Multi-factor authentication, secured communications |

## What Beacon automates today

| Article 21 area | Beacon coverage |
|-----------------|-----------------|
| Risk analysis / policies | Partial — CIS + vuln scores as proxies |
| Incident handling | Incidents register + alert workflow |
| Asset management | Inventory module + fleet visibility |
| Access control / hygiene | CIS checks + privilege/auth alerts |
| Vulnerability handling | Trivy + CVE tracking |
| Security monitoring | Log rules engine + alerts |
| Credential risks | Breach email monitor |

<Warning>
  Gaps remain — business continuity, supply chain questionnaires, training records, and crypto policies require manual evidence outside Beacon today.
</Warning>

## Essential vs important entities

NIS2 classification depends on sector and size under national transposition. Beacon does not determine your entity category — it provides **technical evidence** regardless of classification.

## Using Beacon in an audit

2. Run continuous collection through audit period
3. Export monthly PDF + JSON packs
4. Pair with policy documents and management sign-off (external to Beacon)
